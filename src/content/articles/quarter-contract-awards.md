Three procurement stories defined the quarter, and together they tell a small business exactly what kind of customer the state is right now: one that is spending seriously, contesting bitterly, and being watched more closely than it likes.

## The money that moved: SANRAL in the Eastern Cape

The roads agency awarded R1,4 billion worth of contracts in the Eastern Cape in the weeks from April, spread across road upgrade and maintenance projects. Roadwork awards matter disproportionately to the small business economy because of how they cascade: main contractors must subcontract portions to local SMMEs and employ local labour under standard contract conditions, so every large award seeds dozens of smaller ones in the district around it. If a major road project has been awarded in your area, the practical move is direct: contact the main contractor's site office about subcontracting and supply opportunities (aggregates, plant hire, catering, security, transport) before the site establishes its supplier list.

## The award under fire: SANRAL's R9,5 billion maintenance contract

The quarter's biggest single award, a R9,5 billion road-maintenance contract, went straight into a legal storm, with losing bidders challenging the award and reporting raising questions about a multi-billion rand contract going to the highest bidder. Whatever the courts decide, the lesson for smaller players is about timelines: legally contested mega-awards can freeze work for months. If your business plan depends on subcontracting from a specific award, track the challenge, because "awarded" and "work starting" are different events in South African procurement.

## The award under investigation: Eskom's R21 billion diesel deal

amaBhungane and Daily Maverick reported from inside Eskom's R21 billion contract to supply diesel to the Ankerlig peaking plant, detailing awards to obscure and controversial companies, including roughly R4 billion allocations to firms with question marks over capacity and conduct, while an internal investigation ran for close to a year without published findings. For honest suppliers the signal is double-edged. The bad news: politically exposed mega-contracts remain a rigged game you should not bet a business on. The better news: investigative scrutiny is real and sustained, and the coming Public Procurement Act regime arrives with this case as Exhibit A for why transparency requirements are tightening.

## The backdrop: a R1,07 trillion pipeline

All of this happens inside the largest infrastructure budget in the country's history: R1,07 trillion over three years, with transport taking R417,6 billion and energy R213,6 billion, and more than half the total executed by state-owned companies and public entities. That is the demand side of every tender strategy for the next three years. The quarter's stories show the supply side truthfully: real awards reaching real contractors, big awards attracting big fights, and journalism doing the audit work the system still struggles to do for itself. Position accordingly: chase the cascading work from clean awards, keep clear of the contested ones, and treat compliance as your competitive weapon while the rulebook tightens.
