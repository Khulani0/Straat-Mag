Tender adjudication has two rooms. In the first, officials check compliance: is every required document present, current and correctly signed? In the second, the surviving bids compete on price and preference points. Most SMME bids never reach the second room, and the exits from the first room are all self-inflicted. Bid-readiness means building a folder, once, that walks you through the first room every time.

## The permanent folder

These documents change rarely and should exist before you ever open a tender advert:

- **CIPC registration documents.** Your company registration and director details, matching your other records exactly.
- **CSD registration and your MAAA number.** Registration at csd.gov.za is free and mandatory for government work. The CSD pulls verification from CIPC, SARS and your bank, so errors upstream surface here.
- **SARS tax compliance status pin.** Not a paper certificate: the live TCS pin that lets the buyer check your standing on the day. An expired or non-compliant status disqualifies silently.
- **B-BBEE certificate or sworn affidavit.** Businesses under the turnover thresholds use a free sworn affidavit for their level; larger firms need a verification certificate. Without it you score zero preference points.
- **Bank confirmation letter.** Stamped or digitally issued, matching the CSD record.
- **Company profile and reference letters.** Not formally required, but the difference between a bid and a credible bid, especially for quotation-level work.
- **Sector credentials where relevant.** Construction work requires CIDB registration at the appropriate grade; security requires PSIRA; food requires certificates of acceptability. Know your sector's licence and keep it current.

## The forms that come with every bid: the SBDs

Every tender pack includes National Treasury's Standard Bidding Documents, and they are where careless bidders die. The core set: SBD 1 (the invitation and bid form), SBD 4 (bidder's disclosure, covering interests and past conduct), SBD 6.1 (the preference points claim, which is how your B-BBEE status becomes points), and the contract forms. Depending on the tender you may also see SBD 6.2 (local production and content for designated sectors), SBD 8 (past supply chain practices) and SBD 9 (the certificate of independent bid determination, your sworn statement against bid-rigging).

Three rules keep the SBDs safe. Use the versions in that tender's pack, not last month's, because Treasury revises the forms. Complete every field, writing "not applicable" rather than leaving blanks. And sign everywhere a signature is called for: an unsigned SBD form is an automatic disqualification that no procurement official can rescue, however good your price.

## The bid-day discipline

With the folder built, a live bid becomes assembly rather than panic. Read the tender's own checklist first (most packs list required returnables) and build your submission in that order. Check the compulsory briefing requirement: miss a compulsory site meeting and you are out before submitting. Price from real costs, not from guessing the budget. Deliver early: late is disqualified, with no exceptions, and load-shedding at the print shop is not a defence the system recognises.

Then maintain the folder like a machine: diarise the tax pin, the B-BBEE affidavit's annual renewal, and every licence expiry. The businesses that win consistent government work are rarely the cheapest. They are the ones whose paperwork never loses.
