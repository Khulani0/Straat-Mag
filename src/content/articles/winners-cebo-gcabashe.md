The Anzisha Prize is Africa's biggest award for its youngest entrepreneurs, reserved for founders aged 15 to 22, and its Revenue Growth Award goes to the fellow whose business shows the greatest increase in profitability. In 2025 it went to a South African: Cebolenkosi "Cebo" Gcabashe of Mandeni, KwaZulu-Natal, founder of G Khula Trading.

## One machine, then a company

G Khula Trading started with a single high-pressure cleaner and a 22-year-old determined, in his own words, to make things better. It has grown into a property enhancement business offering deep-cleaning and maintenance to homes and commercial buildings, and the service list has widened as the cash flow allowed: roof cleaning, car washing, upholstery cleaning. The company employs four permanent workers, with Gcabashe aiming for ten, and generates revenue in the tens of thousands of dollars a year, growth strong enough to top the profitability measure across Anzisha's continental fellowship.

"As a person from a rural area, this award means the world to me and the community I come from," Gcabashe said of the win.

The community line is not decoration. Alongside the business, he has offered free training in Mandeni to young people who want to start their own ventures, the kind of quiet multiplier that prize judges notice because it cannot be faked in a pitch.

## What the prize buys

The Revenue Growth Award carries US$10,000, roughly R180,000 at mid-2026 exchange rates, and sits inside Anzisha's two-year venture-building fellowship: coaching, courses, cloud services and stipends, with over US$140,000 distributed across the programme each year. Gcabashe's plans for the capital are unglamorous and exactly right: a bakkie to make the mobile car wash genuinely mobile, a move into solar panel cleaning as the panels multiply on KZN roofs, and a long-term goal of a full auto-valet centre with automated wash bays across the province.

## The lesson in the story

Nothing about G Khula Trading required a tech breakthrough, a patent or a pitch deck with a total addressable market slide. It required a service people already pay for, done reliably, with the profits reinvested in widening the offer. Anzisha did not fund an idea; it rewarded audited growth that already existed. For very young South African founders, the sequence is the strategy: trade first, grow visibly, then let the continent's award programmes amplify what is already working.

Anzisha's next intake opens later this year (applications for the previous cycle closed in November). If you are 22 or under with a trading business, it belongs on your calendar.
