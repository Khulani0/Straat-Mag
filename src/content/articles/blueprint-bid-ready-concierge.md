Every edition, The Blueprint puts one business idea on the table and tests it against real numbers. This is analysis of a market gap, not a profile of an existing company, and not investment advice. Check the assumptions before you act.

## The gap

South Africa plans to spend R1,07 trillion on public infrastructure over the next three years, the largest pipeline of state work in the country's history. That work is legally biased toward small and township businesses through set-asides and subcontracting rules. Yet most township SMMEs never win a rand of it, and the reason is rarely capability. It is paperwork. To bid, a business must be registered on the Central Supplier Database, hold a live SARS tax compliance pin, carry a B-BBEE affidavit, and complete a stack of Standard Bidding Documents without a single unsigned field. Miss one and the bid is thrown out before price is even read. Then, if they win, they wait: at the end of March 2025 government owed suppliers R18,4 billion on invoices already past the 30-day legal deadline, so a small firm can win the work and still be sunk by the wait for payment.

## The idea

A bid-readiness concierge: a local service that gets township businesses compliant and keeps them compliant, then helps them get paid. Three revenue lines:

- **Onboarding.** Register clients on the CSD, sort their tax pin, produce the B-BBEE affidavit, build the permanent document folder. Registration itself is free, so you sell the doing of it, not access.
- **Bid support, per tender.** Assemble and check the SBD pack for a specific bid, so nothing is disqualified on a technicality. Priced per submission.
- **Retainer.** A monthly fee to keep every client's tax pin, affidavit and licences current, and to flag matching tenders as they open.

The paid-late problem opens a fourth line for the ambitious: partnering with an invoice-bridging financier so a client who has delivered can be paid now, against the government invoice, for a fee. That is a regulated activity, so you introduce, you do not lend.

## The numbers to sanity-check

The demand is real and large, but do your own arithmetic before you commit. Estimate how many small businesses in your district could realistically bid, what they would pay for onboarding and per-bid support, and how many bids each would run a year. Your costs are mostly your time plus a laptop, a printer and airtime, so the model lives or dies on volume and repeat retainers, not on any single deal. Government's own agencies (SEDFA, and the TREP programme for township and rural firms) offer some of this support free, so you must be faster, closer and more reliable than a state office to justify a fee. That is a high bar, and an honest one.

## Why it fits a straatpreneur

It needs no stock, no premises and no capital, only knowledge of a system that is public and free to learn, plus the discipline to keep other people's paperwork perfect. It is defensible, because trust and a track record compound, and a client whose bids stop getting disqualified does not leave. And it puts you inside the procurement economy this magazine reports on every week, which means every issue of The Ledger and The Wire is, for you, a lead list.

Explore it, price it honestly, and remember the rule that runs through this whole magazine: the money is real, but only preparation unlocks it.
