South Africans save billions of rand a year through stokvels, and almost none of that money reaches the township businesses trading a street away from where it is collected. SUM1 Investments built its whole model on closing that gap, and in October 2025 the idea won the SAB Foundation's Social Innovation Award and its R1,3 million grand prize.

## The model

SUM1, co-founded by investment professional Kurhula Baloyi, links collective community savings directly to small-scale entrepreneurs through an asset financing model. Instead of a stokvel's pool sitting in a low-interest account, members' capital finances income-generating assets for informal township and rural businesses, the kind of operators traditional banks exclude for lack of collateral and formal records. The returns flow back to the stokvel members. Baloyi, who spent seven years in investment management across private and public markets before building SUM1, designed the investment and risk strategy around what stokvels actually need: capital preservation first, community impact alongside.

"We saw an opportunity where others saw risk," Baloyi said after the win. "By giving stokvels a way to invest in local businesses, we are building a bridge between savings and sustainability and strengthening community economies from within."

## The award

The SAB Foundation's Social Innovation and Disability Empowerment Awards, announced in mid-October 2025, recognise innovations that solve social problems for women, youth, rural communities and persons with disabilities. SUM1 took the top Social Innovation prize of R1,3 million. The parallel Disability Empowerment grand prize, also R1,3 million, went to Blind SA's White Cane project, which has white canes locally produced by blind and partially sighted people themselves, a South African first.

The prize money is only part of what winners receive: the Foundation attaches mentorship, technical support and business development assistance, and its alumni network has become one of the stronger social-enterprise communities in the country.

## Why this matters beyond one company

The financing gap SUM1 targets is the defining constraint of township enterprise: money exists in the community, businesses exist in the community, and the pipes between them do not. A model that turns existing collective savings into productive local capital does not need donor money to scale, it needs trust and track record. That is precisely what a R1,3 million endorsement from one of the country's most credible award programmes buys.

For founders reading this as strategy: SUM1 won with a working model, not a concept. The SAB Foundation's 2026 awards cycle closed for applications in March; the next window is expected in February 2027. If your innovation serves the Foundation's target communities, the time to build your evidence base is now, not the week entries open.
