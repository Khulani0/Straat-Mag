South Africa's longest-running pitching competition crowned a renovator. Gauteng entrepreneur Prudence Mabaso, founder of The Kitchen Wrap Company, won the 2025 Nedbank Pitch and Polish, taking a prize package worth R1 million: R650,000 in cash plus a R350,000 bursary for a two-year business incubation programme at Raizcorp.

## The business

The Kitchen Wrap Company attacks the most hated part of home improvement: the demolition. Instead of ripping out tired kitchen cupboards, the company wraps existing cabinets in premium architectural film, delivering a modern finish with no dust, no demolition and a fraction of the usual downtime. It is renovation re-engineered around the customer's real constraint, which is not money alone but disruption: most households cannot lose their kitchen for six weeks. Mabaso's five-year vision, stated with the win, is to make the company the continent's leading name in premium non-invasive renovation.

## The competition

The 2025 season was the first under Nedbank's banner after years as Engen Pitch and Polish, and it was the programme at full scale: more than 2,200 entries, a televised-style elimination format that drew around 10 million online views, and 16 finalists (plus a wildcard) coached and cut through successive rounds. Pitch and Polish is deliberately built as a training programme wearing a competition's clothes: Raizcorp coaches work on every surviving entrant's pitch between rounds, which is why its alumni consistently describe the process itself as the prize.

## Why this winner is instructive

Three things stand out for founders studying the win. First, the business is unglamorous and precise: a clearly named problem (renovation disruption), a clearly priced solution, and a repeatable service. Judges reward clarity over novelty, and there is nothing to misunderstand about wrapping a kitchen. Second, the prize structure matters more than the headline number: R650,000 in cash is working capital, but the R350,000 incubation bursary is two years of structured support, and competition organisers increasingly weight prizes toward development because cash alone has a poor record of scaling winners. Third, the sponsor change is a strategy lesson in itself: the competition circuit reshuffles constantly, and entrants who track who currently runs what (as this magazine's calendar does) enter the right doors while others chase last year's posters.

Entries for the 2026 season closed on 10 April, with the new season in progress. If The Kitchen Wrap Company's trajectory holds, the 2025 title will look less like a finish line than a funding round: non-dilutive, reputation-rich, and repaid only in execution.
