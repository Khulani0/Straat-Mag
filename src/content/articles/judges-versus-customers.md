South African founders increasingly learn to pitch on the competition stage, and it shows in their sales meetings, where they present five-part frameworks to a shop owner who just wants to know the price and the delivery day. The two audiences reward opposite things, and the founders who win both learn to switch modes deliberately.

## What judges actually score

Competition scorecards are public, and they are checklists. The Township Economy Awards score governance, innovation, community impact, business growth and sustainability. Africa's Business Heroes screens for founder identity, years of operation, traction and African impact before anyone hears a pitch. The InnoVarsity's stated hunt is for ventures solving critical global challenges that can scale efficiently. The SAB Foundation awards innovations that demonstrably serve women, youth, rural communities and persons with disabilities.

Notice the pattern: judges score coverage. A pitch that is excellent on four criteria and silent on the fifth loses to a pitch that is competent on all five, because the scorecard has a row for everything and an empty row scores zero. Judges also carry fiduciary caution: they are spending an institution's money in public, with colleagues watching, so they reward the applicant who removes their risk of embarrassment: compliance, governance, plausible numbers, a founder who will represent the programme well. And on the broadcast stages (Pitch and Polish's last season drew around 10 million online views), the panel is also performing thoughtfulness for an audience, which lengthens deliberation and sharpens the appetite for a clean story arc.

So pitch judges the way the scorecard reads: cover every criterion explicitly, in their vocabulary, with a number attached to each. It feels mechanical because it is. That is the game.

## What customers actually buy

A customer scores nothing. A customer has a problem, a budget and a fear of being made a fool of, and buys relief from the problem at an acceptable price from someone who seems unlikely to disappear. Coverage bores them; a spaza owner does not care about your governance structure, and a procurement officer cares only that your compliance folder lets them defend the award. The customer pitch is narrow and deep: their specific problem, your specific fix, the price, the proof (a reference, a demo, a guarantee), and how little of their time you will consume. Every minute you spend on vision is a minute they spend doubting your focus.

The tell that a founder has confused the modes: they can recite their total addressable market but not their delivery cost to the customer in front of them.

## The switch

Run the two pitches as separate documents and never let them blend. The judge pitch is comprehensive, criterion-ordered, impact-forward. The customer pitch is one page, problem-ordered, price-forward. Competitions are worth entering (we have made that case in this series), but their deepest risk is training you to sound fundable instead of buyable. The correction is a discipline: for every hour rehearsing for a stage, spend one selling to a stranger. Judges hand you a cheque once. Customers hand you one every month, and no scorecard on earth beats that.
