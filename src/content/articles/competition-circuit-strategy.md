Most founders treat pitch competitions as events: something you enter when a poster crosses your WhatsApp. That is the lottery approach, and it loses. The founders who fund real growth from competitions treat the circuit as an annual calendar with known deadlines, known prizes and published judging criteria, and they work it the way a sales team works a pipeline.

## The circuit, verified

These are the major fixtures, confirmed as at early July 2026:

| Competition | Who it is for | What it pays | Cycle |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SAB Foundation Social Innovation Awards | Innovations serving women, youth, rural, disability | R300,000 to R1,3m per award | Opens Feb, closed 16 Mar 2026 |
| Nedbank Pitch and Polish | Any SA entrepreneur | Prize package over R1m | Entries closed 10 Apr 2026 |
| Anzisha Prize | Africans aged 15 to 22 | Share of US$140,000+ pool | Opens around Oct |
| Africa's Business Heroes | African founders, 3+ years trading | Share of US$1,5m | Closed 28 Apr 2026 |
| EDHE Absa InnoVarsity | Students at SA public universities | Up to R1,2m pool | Opened 16 Apr 2026 |
| AWIEF Awards | African women founders | Awards plus profile | Nominations close 3 Aug 2026 |
| Eskom Business Investment Competition | SMMEs trading 24+ months | Business investment prizes | Verify current cycle |

Two patterns jump out of that table. First, the calendar clusters: entries mostly open between October and April. A founder who plans in September enters five competitions; a founder who reacts to posters enters one, late. Second, several fixtures changed recently (Pitch and Polish moved from Engen to Nedbank; the EDHE Intervarsity became the Absa InnoVarsity), so always verify the current sponsor and rules before writing an entry around last year's information.

## Why competitions beat most other early capital

Prize money is non-dilutive and non-repayable: no interest, no equity, no collateral. For a zero-asset founder that makes it the cheapest capital that exists. But the money is often the smaller half of the value. The SAB Foundation attaches mentorship and business development support to its awards. Anzisha wraps a two-year fellowship around its winners. Pitch and Polish is explicitly built as a training programme with a competition attached: every entrant who progresses gets coached on their pitch. Losing well still buys you judge feedback, media exposure and a network of funders who now know your name.

## How to run the pipeline

Build a one-page calendar of every competition you qualify for, with deadlines, and review it monthly. For each, read the judging criteria before you write a word: competitions publish what they score, and an entry that mirrors the scorecard beats a passionate entry that ignores it. Reuse aggressively: your core materials (problem, solution, traction, financials, team) are 80 percent of every entry, so the tenth application costs a fraction of the first. Enter early in your eligibility window, not the final week: several SA competitions do screening interviews on a rolling basis.

And set a rule for your own time. A competition entry that reuses existing material is nearly free; one that demands a custom business plan should clear a bar: expected value (prize times realistic odds) versus a week of selling. The circuit is a funding strategy, not a substitute for customers. The winners we profile in this issue built trading businesses first, then let competitions accelerate them. That order is not a coincidence.
